Picture this. You push into a ruined street, rifle up, heart rate climbing. Somewhere ahead are four enemies. With ESP, you already know where three of them are standing.

That is the whole idea. ESP, often called a wallhack, draws information over your game so you stop guessing and start deciding.

## What each element shows

**Box.** A rectangle around each player. The simplest way to spot someone and judge their size at a glance.

**Name and distance.** Who they are and how far away. Distance matters more than people expect, because it tells you whether to push, hold or flank.

**Health bar.** Is that enemy full health or one shot from dead? That answer changes how you fight.

**Skeleton.** A bone outline. It shows which way someone is facing and whether they are crouched, running or aiming.

**Snapline.** A line from the bottom of your screen to the target. On open maps, it points straight at the squad you lost.

**Player ESP and Bot ESP.** One tracks human players, the other tracks AI. Useful in modes with a lot of bots.

## The mistake everyone makes

Turning on everything. A map with twenty markers, each with a box, name, distance, health bar and skeleton, looks like a spreadsheet. You will spend more time reading than playing.

Do this instead:

1. Start with box and distance only.
2. Add health bar once that feels natural.
3. Use skeleton in tight areas where facing direction matters.
4. Use snaplines on open maps, switch them off indoors.
5. Set max distance so faraway noise disappears.
